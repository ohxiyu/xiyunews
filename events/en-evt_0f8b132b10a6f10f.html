Current outcome

Ethereum Foundation researcher Toni Wahrstätter detailed what ChainSecurity's audit found about EIP-8037 in the Glamsterdam upgrade — gas repricing affecting hardcoded gas-forwarding contracts plus broken invariants where consumed gas can be refunded mid-transaction, skewed gasleft() readings and failed subcalls returning gas — and called on app developers and auditors to check contracts, noting the Hoodi delay bought time but did not solve the issue and that spec changes may ultimately be needed.

  12. #1210.25
    Escalation2026-10-25 22:48 · publication time

    AI agents help rescue ETH’s Glamsterdam fork, ‘Steal My Bitcoin’ site: Hodler’s Digest

    Ethereum's second and final Glamsterdam hard fork testnet on Hoodi was cancelled this week after ChainSecurity found vulnerabilities in the gas-scaling EIPs 8037 and 8038 that could break at least 13 DeFi contracts when a call runs out of gas. Researchers at Eth Labs and the Ethereum Foundation independently spun up AI agents to scan contracts for the flaws, then set up a shared message board in the form of a GitHub repo to coordinate the agents' work. The cancellation postpones the dress rehearsal for an upgrade that Ethereum DeFi operators depend on, and Eth Labs' Derek Chiang said protocols designers should in future do "much more thorough analysis of potential breakages" much earlier in the process. He added that the agent exercise proved effective enough that a second board has been opened to study EIP-8198, or "Quick Slots", proposed for the follow-up Hegota fork. Chiang said the vulnerability was found "a few minutes to midnight," causing everyone to scramble, and the Hoodi testnet is not expected to be rescheduled until all of the fork's second-order effects have been properly analyzed. He likened the setup to the OpenAI–Hugging Face hack, where rogue agents coordinated through a message board, noting "the same pattern is useful for the good agents too."

    Source evidence: Cointelegraph
