Current outcome

The New York Times and a group of other publishers, including the New York Daily News and the Chicago Tribune, filed lawsuits against Brave, the company behind the privacy-focused browser, alleging it scraped their copyrighted content and unlawfully distributed it to AI customers. The complaints, filed Monday, name Anthropic, Perplexity, Mistral, xAI and Cohere as recipients of that content, and also challenge Brave's "Paywall blocker" feature, which the suit says let users bypass publishers' paywalls. The suits target the supply layer of AI search — the index and API through which publishers' journalism reaches model developers — rather than a single model's training run, putting Brave's commercial search API and its paywall-bypass feature directly in legal question. The complaint cites a 2023 Brave guide describing the most common API uses as "training and grounding" large language models, while Brave's current API terms, updated September 1, 2026, bar customers from caching or reselling results or using them to train or improve AI models. As of the filing these are allegations in a complaint; no court has ruled on the claims.
