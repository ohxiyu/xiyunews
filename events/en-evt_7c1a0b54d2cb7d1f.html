Current outcome

Anthropic is developing FlashProve, an open source system that would let anyone verify which AI model produced a given output, according to a company blog post published Tuesday. The company demonstrated the approach using Llama 4 Maverick, a 400-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model, running on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU. The effort targets model-identity and impersonation concerns, aiming to give developers and platforms a way to check that a response genuinely came from the model it claims to originate from. Anthropic describes FlashProve as an open source system, and the demo produced proofs on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU with Llama 4 Maverick. The system is still in development, with no shipped or widely adopted implementation yet.

    Progress on provable inference for large language models

    Anthropic published a research approach, called FlashProve, for provable inference of large Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) language models: the model provider commits to its weights, then issues one zkSNARK proof per request so a client can verify which weights actually served it. Anthropic said it demonstrated the system on a 400-billion-parameter model. The scheme targets a trust gap in hosted model serving — letting a customer confirm which weights ran on a provider's servers rather than relying on the provider's word. Commenters described it as a significant improvement over earlier zero-knowledge results, while noting the roughly 90-100x compute overhead limits near-term practicality. According to discussion of the report, the weight commitment is about 6.5 MB and remains valid for roughly 16 million requests before the provider must commit again, with one proof issued per request. Verifying a proof takes about 17 seconds on 24 threads and 21 seconds on a single thread, including the check from the offline phase.
