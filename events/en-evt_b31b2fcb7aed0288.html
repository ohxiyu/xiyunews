Current outcome

CFTC Chairman Michael Selig said in a Tuesday statement that perpetual futures on individual equity securities can begin trading for the first time on CFTC-registered exchanges, having been deemed approved under CFTC Regulation 40.3. The contracts may be offered on exchanges registered with both the CFTC and the SEC to trade security futures products; Selig did not name which venues would list them or give a start date. Selig framed the move as letting US traders access perpetual futures under domestic safeguards rather than being "forced to turn to offshore, and often unregulated, venues." Kraken responded publicly with "coming soon 👀", signaling commercial follow-through. The announcement follows September filings by Coinbase, Kalshi and Kraken parent Payward (through its Bitnomial exchange), and later OG.com, seeking approval for perpetual futures tied to individual US stocks. On Tuesday afternoon, the Capital Markets Alliance — a coalition including Cboe, Citadel Securities and Healthy Markets — sued to overturn the approvals, arguing the contracts are security-based swaps under SEC jurisdiction and that the CFTC violated the Administrative Procedure Act, and sought to block them from taking effect while the case proceeds.
