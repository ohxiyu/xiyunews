Current outcome

Blockaid said in a post on X that it detected DNS hijacking attacks on the websites of several Web3 projects, including Flap and Stonk, so that visitors are redirected to hacker-controlled pages containing malware. Anyone who reached these project front-ends through the hijacked domains could be routed to attacker infrastructure rather than the genuine site, exposing visitors to malware rather than interacting with the intended application. The compromise described by Blockaid affects the DNS resolution of the projects' websites rather than their on-chain contracts, and Blockaid did not state in the post how many projects or how many users were affected.
