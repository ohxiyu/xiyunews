Current outcome

The fake GIWA Layer 2 bridge received 767.65 ETH from 1,335 addresses, which was drained and funneled through Tornado Cash; DYORSWAP has promised 40% reimbursement to wallets that bridged under 5 ETH, distributed over 200 ETH in compensation, published suspected scammer addresses funded from Binance and Gate, and requested the funds back on-chain.

  1. #0109.28
    Aftermath13:08 UTC · publication time

    DYORSWAP users tricked into sending 767 ETH to fake bridge contract

    More details emerged: DYORSWAP was tricked into integrating the spoofed GIWA chain because it reused the genuine chain ID 9134; 1,335 addresses bridged 767.65 ETH (~$2M), which the scammers drained and funneled through Tornado Cash. Suspected scammer addresses were funded from Binance and Gate, DYORSWAP has distributed over 200 ETH in compensation, and a team-linked address has asked the scammers on-chain to return the funds.

    State after update: The fake GIWA Layer 2 bridge received 767.65 ETH from 1,335 addresses, which was drained and funneled through Tornado Cash; DYORSWAP has promised 40% reimbursement to wallets that bridged under 5 ETH, distributed over 200 ETH in compensation, published suspected scammer addresses funded from Binance and Gate, and requested the funds back on-chain.

    Source evidence: Protos

  2. #0209.28
    Initial12:14 UTC · publication time

    Fake GIWA Layer 2 Bridge Drained of 766 ETH

    A fake GIWA Layer 2 bridge was drained of 766 ETH by an attacker, and DYORSWAP — which mistakenly routed users to the counterfeit network thinking it was the Upbit-backed GIWA project — has promised 40% reimbursement to eligible wallets that bridged less than 5 ETH.

    Source evidence: The Defiant
