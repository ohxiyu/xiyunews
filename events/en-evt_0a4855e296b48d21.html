Current outcome

A researcher has published a demonstration of "weight-space programming," in which an LLM called Astra inspects a neural network's weights directly and edits them to change model behavior, without being allowed to write training loops. In the reported examples, Astra traced a small reasoning model's repetitive looping to a direction written by the MLP layers 15 and 19 and subtracted it from 64 output columns in each layer (131,072 weights), and separately edited a small Qwen model's weights to improve its math performance several-fold without retraining. Commenters on Hacker News described the demonstration as striking evidence that current LLMs may hold some native understanding of their own weight structure rather than requiring external tooling to inspect it. The work is presented as a single-author blog demonstration, not a peer-reviewed or frontier-lab release. According to the write-up, 85 of the base model's first 96 practice answers ran to the 32,768-token limit before the weight edit. The author frames the approach as using the model's reasoning to work out what each part of the network computes, or should compute, and then changing weights accordingly.
