Current outcome

Simon Willison reported that Qwen 3.8 27B, running locally on his laptop, could interpret raw model weights in a replication of an example from the paper "Sparks of Weight-Space Programming: LLMs can natively read & write neural network weights" by Rikiya Takehi, Jayden Teoh, Alex Zhang and Omar Khattab. That paper had demonstrated the capability with frontier models, specifically GPT-6 Astra. The demonstration indicates that no-tools weight reading is not confined to frontier systems such as GPT-6 Astra, since an open-weight model running on a laptop reproduced one of the paper's examples. It remains an exploratory blog-post replication rather than a formal result or peer-reviewed finding. The paper's first experiment trained four neural networks to play 10x10 grid versions of Breakout, Pong, Pac-Man and Freeway, then supplied the weights as floating-point numbers to an LLM with the instruction that it had no tools, no code execution and a single response to identify which game each network was trained on. Willison focused on this no-tools question, noting that critics Matthew Honnibal and Sean Goedecke disputed the paper's framing of code-assisted model editing.
