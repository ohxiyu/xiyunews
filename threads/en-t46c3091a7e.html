    Wells Fargo, Arm and 22 Companies Object to Their Tokenized Stocks Trading on OKXICE Platform

    According to a regulatory filing seen by Reuters, six more companies — including Wells Fargo, Arm Holdings and Intuit — have joined the list of issuers refusing to allow their shares to be tokenized on OKXICE, the joint tokenized-stock venue proposed by OKX and Intercontinental Exchange (ICE). The filing shows 22 companies in total have now objected to the project. The objections add to corporate resistance facing equity tokenization even after US securities regulators recently approved such products, and the filing does not state the companies' specific reasons for objecting. Sixteen of the objecting companies are listed on Nasdaq and six on the NYSE, with Microsoft and Alphabet among those that objected in the prior week. OKXICE co-chair and former New York Governor Andrew Cuomo said the venture expected more objections, and the project had initially proposed 62 companies for tokenized listings when it filed with the SEC earlier in October.

    Nine U.S. Issuers Block Their Shares From Trading on OKXICE Token Platform

    Nine U.S. issuers have moved to block their shares from trading on OKXICE's token platform, restricting tokenized equity trading on the venue. The action targets multiple tokenized equity instruments tied to U.S.-listed companies that the platform intended to offer. The move signals legal and operational risk for tokenized securities venues, as issuer consent can determine whether tokenized shares of a given company remain tradable on a platform. OKXICE is the OKX–ICE tokenized stock venture that filed with the SEC to offer tokenized shares in more than 60 U.S.-listed companies, with one report citing 63 NYSE-listed names, under the agency's Innovation Exemption.
