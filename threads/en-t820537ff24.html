    Coinbase's Third-Party Vendor Problem Strikes Again With Amplitude Leak

    Coinbase acknowledged a security incident at Amplitude, its third-party product analytics provider, which exposed contact information belonging to a small subset of Coinbase customers alongside other Amplitude customers. Coinbase says its own platform was not breached, and the company has pushed back on circulating claims that 70,000 users were affected. The incident extends a pattern the source headline frames as recurring for Coinbase: customer data leaving the exchange through outside vendors rather than through a breach of Coinbase's own systems. Exposed contact details give attackers material for phishing and social-engineering attempts aimed at exchange users. According to The CyberSec Guru, Coinbase disclosed the Amplitude incident on October 28. Reporting also notes that Coinbase's position is that it "has not had a data breach," with the impact attributed to Amplitude and limited to a small subset of customer contact data.

    Coinbase Vendor Incident Exposes The Weak Link Behind Private Keys

    DailyCoin published an analysis arguing that a third-party vendor incident at Coinbase exposes the security weaknesses contractors can introduce to exchange private-key and customer infrastructure. Search excerpts describe the vendor as analytics provider Amplitude, which experienced a security incident affecting a small subset of Coinbase customer contact data, while Coinbase said it found no evidence of unauthorized access to its systems. The episode highlights how third-party vendors can become an indirect exposure path for exchange customer data, even when the exchange says its own systems were not breached. The described exposure involved customer contact data rather than private keys, and Coinbase said funds were unaffected. No loss figure or confirmed private-key compromise has been disclosed.
