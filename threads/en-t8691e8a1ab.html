    Bitcoin redemption freeze puts $136 million Aave market under scrutiny

    A freeze on Bitcoin redemptions has put an Aave lending market holding roughly $136 million in exposure under scrutiny, according to CryptoSlate. The halt raises questions about the solvency of the Bitcoin-pegged asset's issuer and the quality of collateral backing that market. If holders of the affected Bitcoin-pegged asset cannot redeem it for native BTC, the collateral backing loans on that Aave market may be worth less than its on-chain price implies, exposing lenders on that market to potential shortfalls. The $136 million figure refers to the size of the Aave market's exposure, not a confirmed loss. The freeze concerns redemption of a Bitcoin-pegged token used as collateral rather than a failure of Aave itself.

    Aave Freezes tBTC Reserve After Threshold Pauses Minting and Redemptions

    Threshold paused minting and redemptions of tBTC, its wrapped-Bitcoin asset, and Aave responded by freezing its tBTC reserve as a precautionary measure against bad debt and collateral risk. The halt affects a wrapped-Bitcoin asset that is integrated across multiple DeFi venues, including Aave's lending market. Aave's action makes the disruption visible on the largest lending venue where tBTC is used, since a reserve freeze is an asset-specific risk action rather than a routine parameter adjustment. Losses from the Threshold halt have not been quantified. On Aave, a reserve freeze disables new supply and borrow operations for a single market while leaving repayments and withdrawals active, so existing tBTC positions can still be unwound subject to liquidity. Threshold's bridge relies on a decentralized set of node operators using threshold cryptography to mint and redeem tBTC.
