    Intercontinental Exchange (ICE) and trade[XYZ] have announced their plan to use

    Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), the operator of the New York Stock Exchange, and trade[XYZ] announced on Thursday a plan to use Hyperliquid as the infrastructure layer for perpetual futures markets serving US traders across crypto, equities, commodities, foreign exchange and other real-world assets, subject to regulatory approvals. Under the structure, Hyperliquid supplies the underlying technology via HIP-3, trade[XYZ] designs and implements the markets, and ICE operates the regulated market and clearing structure. The arrangement would give US retail traders a regulated route to Hyperliquid-based perpetual futures, while handing ICE a new entry point into retail trading; ICE said the vehicle would run separately from its institutional futures business. OKX CEO Star said the project is being advanced jointly by ICE, trade[XYZ] and OKX, with retail distribution to flow through an ICE-OKX joint venture. ICE said it has signed a definitive agreement with Trade.xyz and will be the exclusive US operator, with a dedicated clearing structure intended to shield its existing customers — more than 99% of its market participation is institutional — from retail counterparty risk. Existing trade[XYZ] HIP-3 markets will remain unchanged, and HIP-3 deployers remain independent operators responsible for their own deployments.

    Moody's assigns investment-grade ratings to Ripple's RLUSD stablecoin

    Moody's Ratings assigned an A2 long-term deposit rating and a P-1 short-term deposit rating to Ripple USD (RLUSD), the dollar-backed stablecoin issued by Ripple subsidiary Standard Custody & Trust Company, the agency said on Tuesday. A2 is the sixth-highest long-term grade on Moody's scale and P-1 is its top short-term grade. Moody's said the ratings reflect the quality of RLUSD's reserves, which are held mainly in Treasury bills, other short-term U.S. government assets and cash, most of it with custodian BNY. The action gives the stablecoin an independent credit assessment at a time when Ripple is pitching it to institutional users. Moody's said RLUSD targets 103% coverage of tokens in circulation, and eligible institutional holders can redeem at par within two business days; the agency also flagged that Standard Custody is not a bankruptcy-remote entity and that RLUSD holders do not currently have a direct ownership interest in the reserves. The ratings were requested by the issuer, and Moody's noted that the GENIUS Act, due to take effect in 2027, will exclude required reserves from an issuer's bankruptcy estate.

    ⚡️Ripple Prime launches RWA perpetual contracts for institutional clients, covering more than 100 markets

    Ripple's prime brokerage arm Ripple Prime is rolling out real-world asset (RWA) perpetual contracts to institutional clients, covering more than 100 reference markets including stocks, indices, ETFs and commodities, according to a Bloomberg report dated Oct. 15. The contracts are offered through Trade.xyz on Hyperliquid and support shared margin across positions, extending Ripple Prime's earlier opening of crypto perpetual trading to clients. Ripple Prime International CEO Michael Higgins said the arrangement can help institutions improve capital efficiency and position management. Clients can pair the perpetual contracts with the underlying assets for basis trades or use them for directional exposure, including during traditional market closures and weekends. Higgins also said traditional hedge funds are "very close" to directly participating in tokenized RWA markets, with the key being infrastructure that meets their counterparty and anti-money-laundering requirements. Over the past year, open interest in RWA perpetual contracts across major trading venues has grown from near zero to $9 billion, according to the report.

    DriveWealth to use Ripple's RLUSD for stablecoin settlement, cross-border payments

    DriveWealth, a brokerage-as-a-service platform, announced a partnership with Ripple to integrate blockchain capabilities — stablecoin settlement, payments, custody and prime brokerage — into its global investment infrastructure. The collaboration focuses first on integrating Ripple's stablecoin infrastructure in Europe, with plans to extend to the United States and other markets. DriveWealth supplies API-based trading infrastructure to banks, fintechs, broker-dealers and consumer brands, so the tie-up would push an existing enterprise stablecoin into brokerage settlement and cross-border funding rails if implemented. The arrangement was announced as a plan rather than a completed integration. The stated scope includes access to Ripple Payments to streamline cross-border fund inflows, digital asset custody in Europe with later expansion to other markets, and exploration of institutional business such as equities financing through Ripple Prime. No timeline, transaction volumes or commercial terms were disclosed.

    Brevan Howard to use Ripple Prime for multi-asset brokerage, clearing and financing

    Ripple Prime will provide multi-asset prime brokerage, clearing and financing services to funds managed by Brevan Howard, a global investment manager with about $35 billion in assets, the firms said in a Tuesday announcement. Brevan Howard chief operating officer Alan McGroarty said the need for institutional-quality digital asset infrastructure is growing as digital and traditional markets become more interconnected. The agreement builds on an existing relationship: Brevan Howard-affiliated funds took part in a $500 million investment in Ripple in 2025 that valued the company at $40 billion. Brevan Howard serves global clients including sovereign wealth funds, corporate and public pension plans, foundations and endowments.
