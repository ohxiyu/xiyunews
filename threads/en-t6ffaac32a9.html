    Standard Chartered launches tokenized deposit service in Hong Kong

    Standard Chartered announced the launch of a tokenized deposit solution for corporate clients in Hong Kong, making the city the first market in its global network to offer the service, with plans to extend it to more markets. Built on the bank's own blockchain infrastructure and developed with support from the HKMA's distributed ledger technology regulatory incubator, the solution relies on EnsembleTX to let tokenized deposits move between participating banks in real time, with interbank settlement in central bank money — including wholesale CBDC — on a 24/7 basis. The service is aimed at corporate clients' intra-group liquidity management and digital treasury and investment workflows, allowing tokenized deposit transfers among participating banks to be settled in central bank money around the clock. BlockBeats reported on November 2, citing CoinDesk, that early partners include China AMC, Fireblocks and HashKey. Hong Kong is currently the only market where the bank offers the service.

    Hong Kong’s Biggest Bank Puts a Stablecoin in 3 Million Wallets

    HSBC launched RedCoin (ticker RCHKD), a Hong Kong dollar stablecoin licensed by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority, on its PayMe app, with more than 3.3 million users able to activate wallets in the coming days to send money to friends or pay at over 100,000 PayMe merchants. The bank said it plans to add the token to its HSBC HK Mobile Banking app later this year. The launch puts an HKMA-licensed stablecoin inside a major global bank's existing consumer wallet, letting Hong Kong users pay with blockchain-based money without opening a crypto exchange — a step beyond the trading-platform venues where stablecoins have mostly circulated. RedCoin runs on HSBC's own private ledger rather than a public blockchain such as Ethereum, and balances cannot move between PayMe and HSBC's banking app; it is also not a bank deposit and is not covered by Hong Kong's Deposit Protection Scheme. HSBC's reserve tracker showed roughly $10.2 million of RedCoin circulating shortly after launch, backed by $10.4 million held entirely in cash, with the first independent attestation of reserves expected after the fourth quarter of 2026. John O'Neill, HSBC's managing director of digital assets and currencies, said the bank will "soon add more products, including expanding into wholesale corporate and institutional use cases."
