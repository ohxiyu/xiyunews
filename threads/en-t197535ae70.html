    Reuters: Deutsche Bank to launch crypto custody in Germany within weeks, expand to Asia next year

    Deutsche Bank plans to launch a digital asset custody service in Germany within weeks, initially supporting BTC and ETH, with stablecoin on/off-ramp services to follow, its corporate bank co-head Gerald Podobnik told Reuters. The bank intends to extend the custody platform to Asian financial centres next year, and separately plans to offer tokenized deposits and help clients issue blockchain-based securities such as digital bonds. A custody offering from Germany's largest bank would add a regulated venue in Europe for institutional BTC and ETH holdings, and the stated roadmap extends beyond custody into tokenized deposits and blockchain-based bond issuance. The launch is still tied to regulatory approval, which the bank has said it expects under MiCA. The rollout is phased: custody of BTC, ETH and selected stablecoins including USDC, EURC and EURAU first, then fiat-to-stablecoin conversion. Podobnik said the move supports the bank's target of 8% compound annual growth in corporate banking by 2028; one report placed the Asia expansion in 2027 rather than "next year", and the bank's planned Asian target markets were not specified.

    Crypto exchange Kraken owner to buy European bank

    Payward, the parent company of crypto exchange Kraken, agreed to acquire Lithuania-based Saldo Bank for an undisclosed amount, a deal that would give it a European Union banking license. The acquisition is subject to regulatory approval and is expected to close in 2027. The deal would let Payward offer EU customers interest-bearing deposits, loans, card services and euro payments, expanding it beyond crypto trading, and would make it the first crypto firm to buy a European bank, according to the Financial Times. Saldo Bank had €163.9 million (about $180 million) in total assets at the end of 2025, up 22.7% year over year, and is licensed in Lithuania, Finland, Sweden and Germany with a passport to operate in the Netherlands; it offers deposits, consumer and business loans and payment services. Payward said its banking business has already originated more than $2 billion in loans and holds a Federal Reserve limited-purpose master account and an electronic money institution license in Ireland.

    Payward Closes Bitnomial Acquisition, Gains Full US Derivatives Licenses

    Payward, the parent company of the Kraken exchange, has closed its acquisition of crypto derivatives platform Bitnomial, giving it a full set of US derivatives licenses covering exchange, clearing and brokerage functions. The deal places a fully CFTC-licensed derivatives stack — designated contract market, derivatives clearing organization and futures commission merchant — inside one of the largest US crypto exchange groups. Kraken has said the combination brings Bitnomial's CFTC-licensed infrastructure together with its client base and distribution across Kraken, NinjaTrader and its wider product family. Reports put the transaction at $550 million in cash and stock. The licenses gained cover the three core US derivatives functions: exchange (DCM), clearing (DCO) and brokerage (FCM).

  • 2026-10-06

    Kraken operator Payward and Singapore Gulf Bank platform for 24/7 institutional crypto settlement

    Payward, the financial infrastructure company behind crypto exchange Kraken, has integrated Singapore Gulf Bank's real-time clearing network, SGB Net, to let a select group of institutional clients in eligible Asian and Gulf jurisdictions settle qualifying digital-asset transactions around the clock and fund trading balances immediately, including on non-banking days. The service launched with U.S. dollar transactions only, and the two firms said they plan to add clients and currencies over time. Payward chief commercial officer Mark Greenberg said the deal targets a structural constraint in crypto markets: assets trade around the clock while the cash backing those trades remains subject to bank cut-off times and multi-day settlement, with exchanges, payment providers, OTC desks and fintechs hitting the same limit. SGB Net, launched in 2025, processes more than $20 billion in fiat transactions each month according to the bank, and Payward said the bank will access digital-asset liquidity through its Kraken Prime prime brokerage service. The firms did not disclose the number of clients in the initial rollout or the transaction volumes involved.
