    OpenAI valued at $1.25 trillion, as rival Anthropic nears its IPO

    OpenAI has raised $11 billion in the first closing of a new funding round, achieving a valuation of $1.25 trillion, with the total round expected to reach up to $20 billion. Meanwhile, its rival Anthropic is nearing its IPO, having publicly filed its S-1 registration statement in October 2026. This valuation makes OpenAI one of the world's most valuable companies, marking a significant capital influx into the AI sector. The development occurs alongside a key rival's progress toward a public listing, highlighting intense competition and investor interest in generative AI. The first tranche of funds reportedly arrived on a Friday, with investors including Coatue Management, T. Rowe Price, SoftBank, Sequoia Capital, Founders Fund, Andreessen Horowitz, and investors from the UAE. OpenAI had initially targeted a $1.4 trillion valuation for at least $30 billion but scaled back its goals after an AI sector selloff in public markets.

    Anthropic still plans to launch IPO before Thanksgiving, valuation up to $1.75 trillion, plans to raise $80 to $100 billion

    According to the Financial Times, Anthropic plans to proceed with its initial public offering before the U.S. Thanksgiving holiday, targeting a valuation range of $1.25 trillion to $1.75 trillion and aiming to raise $80 billion to $100 billion. The company's third-quarter revenue grew nearly 50% to approximately $17 billion, with a latest annualized revenue run rate of about $81 billion. The IPO, if successful, would be one of the largest in history, potentially surpassing SpaceX's record $86.2 billion debut in June 2026. It comes amid a broader sell-off in tech and AI stocks and a reported reduction in OpenAI's funding target, placing significant market pressure on the offering. Some of Anthropic's backers had previously expected a valuation of around $2 trillion or more. The company reportedly plans to invest approximately $900 billion in computing power and infrastructure in the coming years and currently holds nearly $150 billion in cash and liquid investments.

  • 2026-10-29

    VCs will make billions on Anthropic’s IPO, but Amazon is set to make much (much) more

    TechCrunch reported on October 27 that large-scale conversions of convertible notes have revealed the full extent of Amazon's ownership stake in Anthropic, putting the cloud giant in line to earn far more from the AI lab's planned IPO than venture investors. Amazon structured much of its investment as convertible notes — debt that converts into shares upon an IPO or sale — rather than taking stock up front. The disclosures indicate that a corporate backer, not traditional venture firms, holds the largest single financial upside in one of the sector's most anticipated listings. Venture investors including Menlo Ventures, Sequoia, Sound Ventures and Spark Capital are still expected to see billions in returns, according to the report. Amazon has invested billions in Anthropic beyond its initial commitment, a $4 billion investment the company said it had completed. The report attributes the unusually large gap between corporate and venture returns to Anthropic's sheer size, since lead venture investors in a typical tech IPO hold double-digit percentage stakes.

  • 2026-10-28

    Anthropic outranks most IPOs in history

    Anthropic's IPO prospectus, filed on Monday, discloses an expected $1.5 trillion valuation — third-largest in IPO history behind Saudi Aramco's $1.7 trillion and SpaceX's $1.8 trillion — alongside 1,300% six-month revenue growth and a $66.3 billion second-quarter loss. The company says the loss is largely attributable to repriced convertible debt tied to its rising enterprise valuation. The filing provides unusually detailed financial disclosure from a frontier AI lab ahead of what would be one of the largest listings on record, including revenue retention, compute commitments and accumulated deficit figures that are rarely visible for private AI developers. Anthropic reports roughly 900% net revenue retention, but its definition includes customers who paid as little as $1 over a year and excludes Pro and Max subscribers. It also expects to spend $265.6 billion on computational services over the next few years and warns in the filing that it does not expect revenue to keep growing at comparable rates; other coverage of the filing has cited first-half net loss figures in the $80 billion range.

    AI Stocks Selloff Puts Anthropic's $2 Trillion IPO Under Delay Pressure

    Anthropic plans to launch its IPO in mid-November at a target valuation of up to $2 trillion, but a recent selloff in AI-related stocks and a narrowing listing window have made the timetable uncertain, BlockBeats reported on Oct. 24. Under SEC rules a company must publicly file its S-1 at least 15 days before starting its roadshow, meaning Anthropic would need to file by next week to complete a listing in late November. The filing deadline makes the next week a decisive checkpoint: missing it would push a record-sized AI listing past its targeted window. Prediction markets currently put roughly 80% odds on Anthropic listing before the end of the year. The report cites CoreWeave shares falling about 30% in two weeks and Micron down about 17% over the past month, reflecting rising concern about the sustainability of AI capital spending and financing capacity. Some market participants expect Anthropic to postpone the listing for a better environment, while analysts argue its pressing need for compute funding and a potentially harder private fundraising market may push it to proceed.
