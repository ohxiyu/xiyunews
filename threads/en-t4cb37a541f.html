    MSCI Adopts New Index Exclusion Rules; Strategy and Metaplanet Face Potential Re

    MSCI has adopted new rules to exclude non-operating companies from its Global Investable Market Indexes. In an earlier simulation based on May 2026 data, MSCI identified Bitcoin treasury companies Strategy (MSTR) and Metaplanet (3350) as potential removals, while Ethereum treasury company SharpLink (SBET) would be placed on a watchlist; the final list of affected constituents is due November 11, with the rules effective after the November 30 close. Index exclusion would force passive funds tracking MSCI's Global Investable Market Indexes to divest the largest listed bitcoin-treasury equities, and it signals a tightening stance toward digital-asset treasury vehicles. Exclusion requires a company to fail an operating-assets test and trigger at least four of five financial exclusion criteria, and existing constituents must fail the screening in both of their two most recent annual filing periods before removal. MSCI will also lift temporary index restrictions on companies previously identified as holding digital assets worth at least 50% of total assets, assessing them under the new methodology instead.

    Strategy’s USD Cash down 60% in seven weeks

    Strategy’s USD Cash fell 60% from $1.61 billion on August 30 to $640.5 million on October 11, according to the company’s latest disclosure this week. Most of the spending went to buybacks of STRC, its high-yield dividend preferred stock, which had briefly collapsed to $71.25 from a $90–100.03 offering range. The buybacks consumed cash that Strategy had mostly raised by diluting MSTR common shareholders, and at the disclosed pace its USD Cash would run out in roughly four weeks if not topped up. The company is the largest corporate bitcoin treasury holder, so its dollar liquidity bears on how much room it has to keep accumulating BTC. Strategy’s August USD Cash figure included expected, not-yet-settled stock sale proceeds, which the company footnoted. A separate “USD Reserve,” earmarked for bond coupons and dividends, stood at $4.88 billion on October 11, down from $5.10 billion on August 30 — a combined decline of about 18%. Strategy’s board doubled the STRC buyback authorization to $2 billion in September, with just $354.8 million of that remaining as of October 11.

    Strategy opts for bigger spending on STRC buybacks over BTC purchases

    Strategy bought 334 Bitcoin for $28.7 million last week while spending $176.3 million to repurchase roughly 1.77 million shares of its STRC preferred stock, according to a Monday 8-K filing with the SEC, bringing its Bitcoin holdings to exactly 848,000 BTC. In a separate proxy filing the same day, the company asked shareholders to approve paying regular dividends on its STRC, STRF, STRK and STRD preferred shares every business day, replacing STRC's twice-monthly schedule and quarterly payments on the other three. The filing shows a capital-allocation shift at the largest corporate Bitcoin holder, where weekly spending on preferred-stock buybacks was more than six times its Bitcoin purchase budget. Strategy said the dividend-timing proposal would not change dividend rates or its overall payment obligations, but could shorten reinvestment delays and improve liquidity and price stability. Shareholders will vote on the dividend proposal at a special meeting on Oct. 28; if approved, STRC's daily schedule would begin in November and STRF, STRK and STRD would follow in January. Strategy's Bitcoin holdings rose just 0.2% in the third quarter, from 846,000 BTC at the end of June to 847,666 BTC at the end of September, as it acquired 7,218 BTC and sold 5,553 BTC during the period, and it spent about $1.38 billion repurchasing STRC over the quarter.

    Strategy has burned through 64% of its STRC rescue fund

    Strategy has spent $1.28 billion, or 64%, of its $2 billion Digital Credit Securities Repurchase Program on buying back STRC, leaving less than $724 million as of Monday's filing. STRC, which Strategy has said it wants to trade near its $100 stated amount, opened for trading at $99.18. The fund meant to keep STRC near $100 is nearly depleted, while the shares have traded below $99 on the majority of trading days over the past year. The buybacks are funded largely by dilution of MSTR common stock, which ranks junior to STRC. Strategy spent $151.7 million repurchasing STRC last week at an average price of $98.86 per share, up from an $86.52 average in late July, and has bought back 13.3 million STRC shares, roughly one in eight outstanding. Although the program technically allows repurchases of STRF, STRD and STRK, the source reports it has only ever repurchased STRC.
