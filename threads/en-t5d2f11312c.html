    Chainalysis Confirms Security Breach, Customer Details Exposed

    Chainalysis has confirmed a security breach in which customer details were exposed, CryptoRank reported. The disclosure makes the blockchain-analytics and compliance vendor the latest third-party provider in the crypto industry to acknowledge a compromise of client information. Chainalysis supplies transaction-tracing and compliance tooling to exchanges, financial institutions and government agencies, so exposure of its customer details raises data-protection and counterparty-risk questions for those clients. The report describes the impact as confined to customer details rather than any loss of funds, with the confirmed scope of exposed data described as limited.

    Bitcoin Wallet Maker Keystone Pulls Devices as Stolen Key Opens Door to Fakes

    Keystone said Monday it will destroy 18,835 unsold hardware wallets and has suspended shipping of all unshipped website orders after an attacker obtained the permanent factory WebAuth key its devices use to prove they are genuine. The breach, detected October 20, also exposed 25,964 buyer records from Keystone's Shopify store covering 2024 to 2026, including names, contact details, shipping addresses and order information. Because the stolen key is what Keystone's own verification page relies on, counterfeit devices could pass the authenticity check and be sold to users who load real savings onto them; Keystone said such devices could be used unknowingly as genuine products, "potentially resulting in asset theft." The company says recovery phrases and private keys were not involved and that devices delivered before October 20 are unaffected. Keystone says 51,083 buyer records were involved in total, but 25,119 had already had personal information erased, leaving 25,964 records that still held data — a retention failure the company attributed to automated bulk-deletion tasks that failed without being detected in time against its 180-day erasure policy. Purchases through Amazon and other channels were not affected; website sales will switch to preorders until devices with a new key are produced, and Keystone says it has filed reports with authorities in the EU, UK and other jurisdictions while warning that anyone claiming to run a "recall" and asking users to ship back devices is a scammer.

    Keystone Breach Exposed 25,964 Purchase Records and Compromised Device Verificat

    Keystone said its investigation found that attackers who breached its website obtained the WebAuth device-authentication key used to verify that a hardware wallet is genuine, potentially allowing counterfeit devices to pass official authenticity checks. The company will destroy 18,835 unsold devices built with the old key, pause unshipped orders and move new production to a replacement key, while the incident affected 25,964 purchase records and 12,873 incomplete checkout records. The compromised key undermines the authenticity check customers rely on to confirm a device is genuine, even though Keystone said seed phrases, private keys and transaction signing were not compromised. The exposed purchase data — names, contact details, shipping addresses and order details — creates phishing risk, and one customer had already reported a social-engineering call that used Keystone order information. Keystone said the affected records were limited to purchases made through Shopify on its official site, and that its automated data-deletion tool "did not operate reliably," so some personal information was retained beyond the promised period; it also said another roughly 25,000 records had already been permanently erased under its retention policy.

    Keystone customers say breach exposed data the hardware wallet maker promised to delete

    Customers of hardware wallet maker Keystone say a data breach exposed personal information that the company had previously pledged to delete, according to a report by The Block published on Oct. 25, 2026. The accounts describe a leak of customer records rather than of wallet keys or on-device funds. The exposure creates phishing and physical-security risk for self-custody users, whose contact and delivery information can be used to target them directly, according to the report. It also undercuts a data-retention promise made by a vendor whose product is built around user-controlled security. The complaint centers on data that customers say Keystone had told them would be erased, and the reported material relates to customer contact and shipping details rather than the air-gapped signing devices themselves.
