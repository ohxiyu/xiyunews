data-context-placement="background" open><summary>Background</summary><div class="story-more-content"><section data-context-kind="background"><h3>Background</h3><p>FlashProve involves two parties: the model provider as prover and the user as verifier. Anthropic said the work advances zkSNARKs, a class of cryptographic proof it describes as uniquely suited to provable inference because of their hiding and efficiency properties.</p></section></div></details><div class="story-summary-body"><p>Anthropic published a research approach, called FlashProve, for provable inference of large Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) language models: the model provider commits to its weights, then issues one zkSNARK proof per request so a client can verify which weights actually served it. Anthropic said it demonstrated the system on a 400-billion-parameter model.</p><p>The scheme targets a trust gap in hosted model serving — letting a customer confirm which weights ran on a provider&#x27;s servers rather than relying on the provider&#x27;s word. Commenters described it as a significant improvement over earlier zero-knowledge results, while noting the roughly 90-100x compute overhead limits near-term practicality.</p><p>According to discussion of the report, the weight commitment is about 6.5 MB and remains valid for roughly 16 million requests before the provider must commit again, with one proof issued per request. Verifying a proof takes about 17 seconds on 24 threads and 21 seconds on a single thread, including the check from the offline phase.</p></div><p class="source-line">hackernews · <a class="source-link" href="https://www.anthropic.com/research/provable-inference" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ltononro</a> · <time datetime="2026-11-05T01:17:46+08:00">Nov 5, 01:17</time> · <a href="https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50368626" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Discussion</a> · <span class="provenance">Single source</span></p><details class="story-more" data-context-placement="after"><summary>Analysis and references</summary><div class="story-more-content"><section><h3>Impact analysis</h3><p>The crypto transmission path runs through zero-knowledge proof infrastructure: FlashProve relies on zkSNARKs, the same proof systems used by zkML and verifiable-compute networks, so progress on proving large-model inference bears on how those projects frame feasibility and cost. The 90-100x overhead described in discussion suggests the technique is not yet a practical substitute for existing serving or verification designs, and no token, venue or protocol is directly implicated by the research itself.</p></section><section><h3>Discussion</h3><p>A commenter who said they developed prior work called VerInf, which the approach builds on, said the design including the choice of a 400B Maverick model came from that work with little attribution.
