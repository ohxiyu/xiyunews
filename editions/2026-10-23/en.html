CVE-2026-111943: Compromised Intermediate Certificate Authority

Background

Background

eMudhra is an India-based certificate authority that sells certificates under the emSign brand through companies in India, the United States and the Netherlands, and its roots are included in the Mozilla, Chrome and Apple root stores. According to The Register, it has issued more than 60 million certificates. Reports describe the flaw as originating in an HSM key-wrapping test script that produced a second set of weak P-384 key pairs during the April 2025 ceremony.

NIST's National Vulnerability Database published CVE-2026-111943 for a compromise of eMudhra intermediate certificate authority certificates, after Mozilla and Google's Chrome Root Program confirmed on October 20, 2026 that the private keys for eight eMudhra intermediates could be recovered directly from their public certificates. Chrome responded by removing emSign ECC Root CA - G3 from the Chrome Root Store to protect consumers that do not take CRLSet updates.

Because the private keys can be derived by anyone from public material, an attacker could mint a certificate that appears valid for any domain and impersonate sites such as banks, GitHub or PyPI, forcing operating system and browser vendors to push emergency trust-store updates.

eMudhra said in its incident statement that the affected intermediates were created during a key-generation ceremony in April 2025 and that its root CA private keys and other intermediate authorities were not affected, adding that no end-entity certificates were issued. Mozilla reported that it independently recovered the private keys for the impacted certificates at 21:48 UTC, confirming the key-generation process was flawed and trivial to exploit.

hackernews · keks24 · Oct 22, 16:51 · Discussion · Single source

Analysis and references

Impact analysis

The exposure runs through the web PKI trust layer: any crypto exchange, custodian, wallet provider or dApp front end that relies on standard HTTPS certificates or certificate-based service authentication may need to reissue certificates or update trust stores, and clients that fail to apply updates face impersonation risk in the interim.

Discussion

Commenters focused on operational fallout: Gentoo users were advised to upgrade app-misc/ca-certificates to 20260816.3.125 or higher, and one noted Red Hat customers still had only a workaround disabling two Comodo root certificates used to sign the eMudhra certificates, with the advisory marked ongoing. Others flagged that Python users may need separate steps, while one commenter argued the underlying eMudhra incident is older news that NIST is only now formally cataloguing.

Solana’s biggest network change since 2020 is live. Here’s how users benefit

Background

Background

Before Alpenglow, Solana relied on TowerBFT for consensus and Turbine for block propagation, alongside Proof of History. Votor is described as a stateless, explicit voting structure inspired by the Streamlet protocol, designed for fast finality with minimal complexity. Alpenglow is described as the biggest overhaul of Solana's consensus mechanism since the network's 2020 mainnet launch.

Solana activated its Alpenglow consensus overhaul on mainnet, replacing the TowerBFT consensus mechanism with Votor and the Turbine block-propagation protocol with Rotor. The change cuts block finality from roughly 12.8 seconds to about 150 milliseconds.

According to Solana, Alpenglow is the network's first major consensus replacement since TowerBFT, changing how validators vote and how blocks reach finality. A finality time of roughly 150 milliseconds instead of about 12.8 seconds directly shortens the wait for transaction confirmation on the network.

Solana states the upgrade leaves transaction execution and the SVM unchanged, altering only consensus-related mechanics and how consensus information is exposed to downstream infrastructure. Rotor, like Turbine, erasure-codes each block into smaller "shreds" that can be reconstructed from a subset.

Analysis and references

Impact analysis

Solana is one of the largest layer-1 networks, so cutting finality to roughly 150 milliseconds affects the confirmation workflows that exchanges, bridges and DeFi applications build around SOL deposits, withdrawals and settlement. The upgrade is a change to network infrastructure and user experience rather than to token supply or custody arrangements.

Background

Uniswap Labs said in an Oct. 22 post that Aggregator Hooks are now live, making Uniswap v4 function as an aggregator by wrapping external liquidity sources into v4 pools that any router can treat as a Uniswap pool. Swaps routed through those external sources pay the protocol fee and contribute to UNI burn, linking aggregator volume to UNI supply.

Per block, the router pulls the latest quotes from integrated sources, builds the best path between Uniswap pools and external venues, and settles within a single transaction without requiring new approvals. BlockBeats reported the launch on Oct. 22.
