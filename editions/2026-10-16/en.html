Morning Minute: Solana Is About To Get Faster

Background

Background

Alpenglow is described as Solana's first major consensus replacement since TowerBFT, changing how validators vote, how blocks reach finality and how consensus information is exposed to downstream infrastructure, while leaving transaction execution and the SVM unchanged. Anza is a Solana-focused research and development lab, founded in January 2023, that engineers the network's core protocol.

Solana has scheduled the mainnet activation of Alpenglow for October 21 at around 15:00 UTC, retiring the consensus mechanism it has run on since launch. Anza, the core development shop behind the upgrade, says finality will drop from roughly 12.8 seconds to a target of about 150 milliseconds — about 85 times faster.

Finality — the moment a transaction stops being reversible — has forced every exchange and payment processor to build around a roughly 13-second window, and the upgrade is intended to close it. The new mechanism is described as designed to survive 20% of stake acting maliciously plus another 20% simply offline, whereas the old system breaks if more than about a third of staked SOL turns hostile.

Because validators will vote directly to each other instead of casting votes as on-chain transactions, Solana's headline transaction count and TPS figures are expected to fall sharply even though users, volume and apps are unchanged. The upgrade is phase one of two, with no date yet for the second phase, and a previously announced September 28 activation date was announced and then pushed.

Impact analysis

Faster finality shortens the settlement window that exchanges, custodians and payment processors must manage — a channel that touches DeFi, MEV and validator economics on a network carrying a large share of on-chain activity. The announcement lands during a broader macro-driven selloff in which majors fell 3-5%, including SOL at $102, down 5%, with over $900M in crypto longs liquidated in 24 hours.

Welcome Muse Spark Open: open-weight frontier multimodal mixture-of-experts model from Meta

Background

Background

Open-weight models publish their trained parameters so anyone can download and run them on their own hardware rather than only through the developer's online service, with Meta's Llama family among the best-known examples. MLX is Apple's open-source array framework for machine learning on Apple silicon, first released in December 2023; Meta's previous model-family releases have also paired weights with named runtimes.

Meta announced Muse Spark Open, an open-weight frontier multimodal mixture-of-experts model that it describes as "the most capable US open-weight model to date." Meta says it is the first model in its class that can run on a desktop using two Apple M5 Ultra Mac Studios with the latest MLX framework, and the Hugging Face listing names it Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B.

Because the weights are published, frontier-class multimodal parameters can be downloaded, fine-tuned and self-hosted rather than accessed only through Meta's services. Meta also frames Apple silicon as a target deployment platform, presenting local desktop inference as viable for a model of this class.

Meta and Apple teamed up for the launch, with the model optimized to run locally on Apple silicon; a community-linked Meta deployment page lists support across multiple model runners, including Unsloth and MLX. The published model identifier is Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B.

Background

Background

MSCI has adopted new rules to exclude non-operating companies from its Global Investable Market Indexes. In an earlier simulation based on May 2026 data, MSCI identified Bitcoin treasury companies Strategy (MSTR) and Metaplanet (3350) as potential removals, while Ethereum treasury company SharpLink (SBET) would be placed on a watchlist; the final list of affected constituents is due November 11, with the rules effective after the November 30 close.

Index exclusion would force passive funds tracking MSCI's Global Investable Market Indexes to divest the largest listed bitcoin-treasury equities, and it signals a tightening stance toward digital-asset treasury vehicles.

Exclusion requires a company to fail an operating-assets test and trigger at least four of five financial exclusion criteria, and existing constituents must fail the screening in both of their two most recent annual filing periods before removal. MSCI will also lift temporary index restrictions on companies previously identified as holding digital assets worth at least 50% of total assets, assessing them under the new methodology instead.

Background

Background

A liquidation is the forced, automatic closure of a leveraged position once losses push collateral below the exchange's maintenance margin, and the resulting forced selling can drag prices lower and trigger further liquidations. The trigger this time was inflation data: September consumer prices rose 3.7% year over year against a 3.6% forecast, with core inflation at 2.6% versus 2.5%, while Thursday's Producer Price Index rose 6.3% annually versus 5.5% expected. Kalchev framed the selloff as a broad exit from risk assets rather than purely a Fed story, citing falling stocks and copper, rising gold and a roughly 8% drop in South Korea's Kospi.

Bitcoin fell below $80,000 on Thursday for the first time in over a month, extending Wednesday's inflation-driven slide. CoinGlass data cited by Decrypt shows $948.22 million in crypto liquidations over 24 hours across 177,440 traders, with long positions accounting for $858.96 million, or 90.6%.

The move put U.S. spot Bitcoin ETF holders back underwater: Bitcoin is trading roughly 2% below that cohort's average cost basis near $82,300, a level it had closed above on 21 of the 24 days since September 21. Nexo analyst Iliya Kalchev called reclaiming that line "the first test of any rebound."

Ethereum led liquidations at $282.03 million, with Bitcoin just over $200 million, and the day ranked third-heaviest for long liquidations in 90 days behind October 8 and August 22. The largest single order was a $12.03 million Bitcoin long on Binance force-closed at $80,208.96; Bitcoin hit a low of $79,711 before buyers pulled it back near $80,925, with the RSI around 44.
