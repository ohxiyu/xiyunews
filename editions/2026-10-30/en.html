Binance investigating hack as suspicious outflows reach up to $5.8 billion

Background

Background

The incident, reported on Oct. 29, 2026, is being tracked through public block explorers across ten wallets, eight of which are labeled as exploiters. Stablecoin issuers can blacklist addresses because their tokens are centrally issued, whereas ETH has no issuer able to freeze it, which is why attackers swapped freezable stablecoins into ether. Binance operates the SAFU (Secure Asset Fund for Users) insurance fund, which a community commenter valued at roughly $1 billion in BTC.

Binance said it is investigating a security incident after wallets linked to the exchange were drained of at least $5.5 billion on Thursday, with on-chain tallies cited by The Block putting suspicious outflows at up to $5.8 billion across Ethereum, Bitcoin and Tron. Binance has not confirmed that user funds were affected, though security researchers widely describe the incident as a hack that would be the largest in the industry's history.

The affected venue is the largest centralized exchange by trading volume, so unresolved questions about the scale of losses and whether customer balances are impaired bear directly on how traders price counterparty and custody risk across the market. Bloomberg reported that the suspected hack triggered emergency asset freezes and sent digital asset prices tumbling.

Tether blacklisted 975.1 million USDT and Circle blacklisted 823 million USDC in the main receiving wallet, and Binance co-CEO Yi He said Ripple also froze about $200 million in RLUSD. The attacker routed funds through Uniswap contracts and borrowed ETH on Aave against USDT collateral — roughly 253,000 ETH (about $570 million) sat in two wallets with no outgoing transfers as of 22:07 UTC — while Aave froze its V3 markets on Ethereum mainnet and Solana-based Kamino paused borrow markets as a precaution.

Singapore Police: Some Retail-Purchased Ledger Hardware Wallets Contained Malicious Components, Total Losses About S$3.5 Million

Background

Background

Ledger is a French hardware wallet maker founded in Paris in 2014; its devices store private keys offline. A recovery phrase, also called a seed phrase, is the backup that can restore a non-custodial wallet and all its keys on another device, which is why capturing it defeats the purpose of a hardware wallet. Supply-chain attacks target the less-secure links around a product — such as distribution and retail — rather than the manufacturer's own systems.

Singapore police said on October 28 that some Ledger hardware wallets bought from retail stores in the country contained malicious components able to capture users' recovery phrases or private keys. At least two cases have been reported since September 10, 2026, with combined losses of about S$3.5 million (roughly US$2.7 million).

The affected devices are sold through ordinary retail channels, so users who believe they hold keys offline may still have had their recovery phrases exposed. Police said the unauthorized parties used the captured data to access wallets and move assets without the owners' knowledge or consent.

Singapore police said they have contacted Ledger, which is reviewing the affected cases, and that investigations are ongoing. The announcement did not specify how many devices were affected or which retailers were involved.

Impact analysis
