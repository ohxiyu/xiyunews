Ripple freezes about $200 million in RLUSD as hackers shift to DEXes

Background

Background

RLUSD is Ripple's U.S. dollar-pegged stablecoin, which like other issuer-controlled stablecoins can be frozen at the token level. Kamino is an open-source credit and liquidity protocol on Solana that lets users lend and borrow against crypto collateral. Issuer freezes of suspected hack proceeds have become a standard post-exploit response in crypto.

According to Decrypt, Binance co-CEO Yi He said Ripple has frozen approximately $200 million of RLUSD. Suspected attackers are using Uniswap and Aave to swap freezable stablecoins into ETH, and Kamino has paused borrowing in its SOL/BTC markets.

The freeze shows an issuer using its blacklist capability at scale against suspected exploit proceeds, while the attackers' move into ETH via DEXes and lending markets illustrates how freezable stablecoins are being converted into assets issuers cannot freeze. Kamino's halt shows the response extending into Solana lending markets.

The figure comes from Binance co-CEO Yi He as cited by Decrypt, and full loss attribution remains emerging. Kamino's pause applies to borrowing in its SOL/BTC markets.

Trezor reveals Malaysian reseller sold wallet with spyware

Background

Background

Trezor dropped CryptoBilis as an official reseller on October 9 following an $86 million Ledger wallet hack that relied on spyware installed in CryptoBilis-sold wallets; Ledger subsequently cut ties with official resellers across Malaysia, Indonesia, Singapore, Thailand, the Philippines, Vietnam and Hong Kong. At the time of the Ledger hack, Trezor said there was "not a single piece of evidence that Trezor devices have been compromised." Trezor says it is now reviewing its other Asia-based resellers.

Trezor said a Safe 5 hardware wallet purchased from its official Malaysian reseller CryptoBilis in August contained a physical spyware implant — an extra circuit board fitted between the main board and the screen, carrying a cellular module and antenna. According to the user report, the implant reads what the screen displays, including wallet backups, and transmits it over a mobile network whenever the device is plugged in; Trezor has not yet examined the device in its lab but is treating the report as credible and will replace affected wallets.

Trezor's chief commercial officer Danny Sanders said, "We haven't had the device in our lab yet, but we're treating the report as credible, and we'd rather warn people now than wait for confirmation and find out someone lost their funds in the meantime." Buyers who purchased a wallet from CryptoBilis since March 2026 were told to move their funds out and contact Trezor for a replacement.

Trezor said it checked four other CryptoBilis-sourced Trezor wallets and "found no evidence of tampering," and it maintained that "no funds have been reported as stolen" in connection with this report. The described implant works similarly to the spyware previously found in Ledger devices.

It affects self-custody holders of Trezor and competing hardware wallets through the custody-integrity channel rather than through price, and keeps supply-chain verification in focus for the sector.

Bitcoin Trades Below $80,000 as Long Liquidations Hit $1.34 Billion

Background

Background

In crypto derivatives, liquidation is the automatic closure of a leveraged position when the trader's margin is no longer sufficient to support the trade. Long liquidations hit traders betting on rising prices when prices fall. Sharp declines can trigger cascading forced selling as more leveraged positions are closed.

Bitcoin traded below $80,000 as long liquidations reached $1.34 billion, according to The Defiant, reflecting a sharp market-wide deleveraging across crypto derivatives.

The event marks a material market-wide deleveraging in crypto derivatives, with leveraged long positions forced out as bitcoin fell below the $80,000 level.

The $1.34 billion figure refers to long positions, meaning traders betting on higher prices were liquidated during the drop.

Impact analysis

Bitcoin's role as the market's primary collateral and benchmark asset can also transmit the deleveraging effect to the broader crypto market.

Intel Stock Suffers Worst Crash in 50 Years as Weak Forecast Overshadows AI Chip Demand

Background

Background

Intel is trying to rebuild its manufacturing business and compete with Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company as a contract chipmaker, a capital-intensive effort that is consuming cash. In September 2025, Nvidia invested $5 billion in Intel and agreed to collaborate on custom processors for PCs and data centers. Industry tracking of advanced packaging, such as CoWoS and flip-chip formats, has flagged lead times extending beyond 52 weeks.

Intel shares fell nearly 30% to $65.76 on Friday, the steepest single-day decline in more than 50 years, wiping out over $146 billion in market value. The drop followed fourth-quarter guidance of $15–16 billion in revenue (midpoint $15.5 billion) and adjusted earnings per share of $0.25, versus analyst estimates of $17.07 billion and $0.43.

Intel said demand for its AI server processors is booming but that manufacturing and packaging shortages are limiting how many chips it can produce, so the AI infrastructure buildout is not translating into near-term revenue. The company also said it expects the PC market to contract through 2027.

Third-quarter results beat guidance: revenue rose 21% year over year to $16.5 billion and adjusted EPS reached $0.52 versus the $0.40 Street estimate, with the Data Center and AI division generating $7.3 billion, up 78%. Intel guided fourth-quarter non-GAAP gross margin down to 38% from 44.1%, and CFO Dave Zinsner cited weaker PC demand, manufacturing constraints on server processors and an accident at a factory in Israel, while warning Intel probably will not generate positive free cash flow next year.

Binance Hack Shows Why Offshore Exchanges Need US Oversight, CFTC Chair Says

Background

Background

The CFTC published an Advanced Notice of Proposed Rulemaking on Oct. 5, 2026, seeking public comment on a fit-for-purpose framework for crypto-asset retail commodity transactions, with comments due Oct. 20, 2026. The agency has pressed ahead with its own rulemaking after the Clarity Act stalled in the Senate, and has separately moved to clarify rules on U.S. access to offshore crypto exchanges.

CFTC Chairman Michael Selig told CNBC on Friday that the roughly $6.4 billion Binance hack "proves our exact point" in support of the crypto-market rules the agency proposed earlier this month, pitching a "federal option" that would let offshore venues register with the CFTC and meet the same cybersecurity standards as U.S. markets. Binance, which initially blamed a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop, said its investigation had since uncovered "additional points of breach," with CEO Richard Teng describing a "more complex attack process" that "did not simply use one engineer's permissions."

The remarks hand the CFTC a concrete, record-setting case as it advances its own crypto rulemaking instead of waiting on Congress. Selig argued that on-chain traceability gives regulators leverage even when obfuscation tools are used.

Selig said the exploit did not break Ethereum or its smart contracts but a vulnerability he believed was "introduced from someone's laptop," though the attack partly played out on-chain: the attacker used EIP-7702 to turn a Binance hot wallet into a "sweeper" with 50 pre-signed authorizations. TRM Labs said the broader breach points to a compromise of Binance's signing infrastructure rather than a blockchain weakness; Binance has set aside $6.34 billion to cover losses, the Secret Service and FBI have opened a probe, and TRM Labs and Chainalysis tied the theft to North Korea's TraderTraitor group.

Analysis and references

Impact analysis

The transmission path runs through market access and custody standards: if offshore venues can register with the CFTC under a federal option, the terms on which U.S. users reach offshore liquidity, and the cybersecurity and signing-infrastructure requirements those venues face, would both change. The proposal remains a proposal, so any effect on venue eligibility or liquidity is contingent on rulemaking that has not been finalized.

A $10 sports bet helped trigger 13 felony charges against Kalshi in Utah

Background

Background

Kalshi is a CFTC-regulated exchange where users trade event contracts on real-world outcomes, including sports. Utah bans gambling outright, and unlike other states without legal sportsbooks, federally regulated prediction markets have not functioned there as a de facto alternative. Sports contracts now account for the majority of volume on regulated prediction-market exchanges.

Utah prosecutors filed criminal charges against prediction-market operator Kalshi and two of its co-founders, with the company and each executive facing 13 felony counts and one misdemeanor for violating the state's gambling laws, according to Ars Technica and the Associated Press. The case is tied to a $10 sports-related event contract, escalating a legal conflict over Kalshi's sports offerings.

The charges mark a new front in the dispute over who can regulate event contracts: Kalshi operates under CFTC oversight federally, while Utah enforces its own strict anti-gambling statutes. Ars Technica reported that Utah had already defeated Kalshi in federal court after the company filed a preemptive suit seeking to block enforcement of state gambling law.

The charges were filed on a Wednesday, months after a federal judge rejected Kalshi's effort to bar Utah from enforcing its gambling laws, per the AP. Ars Technica reported that the Trump administration has separately sued Utah over the state's total ban on gambling in an effort to protect Kalshi.

Impact analysis

The action is a state-level criminal enforcement step, not a nationwide market-structure ruling, so its immediate market channel is access: prediction-market platforms could face gambling-law exposure in states that ban betting, which affects the breadth of sports event-contract liquidity. Crypto-linked prediction markets share that regulatory-overhang sentiment, since state gambling enforcement and CFTC jurisdiction remain unresolved.
