Trezor Cuts Its Resellers After Ledger Finds Spy Implant in Crypto Wallets

Background

Background

CryptoBilis is listed as an official Ledger reseller in Indonesia, Malaysia and the Philippines, and Ledger asked the company to pause all sales and shipments during the investigation. In May 2022, Trezor warned that counterfeit Model T wallets were being sold on a Russian marketplace by a non-authorized seller, with opened, resealed devices whose recovery phrases were predictable or predefined; its advice then was to buy only from its official shop or an authorized reseller. Trezor also said earlier this week that CryptoBilis was already off its seller list and that it had received no reports of tampered Trezor devices from that retailer.

Trezor has told partners in its authorized reseller network that their authorization ends on January 1, 2027, and that it will ship them no new stock, days after rival Ledger's Donjon security team confirmed a hidden spy implant in hardware wallets sold by Southeast Asian reseller CryptoBilis. Trezor said there is "not a single piece of evidence" its own devices were compromised and described the cut as a precaution.

The Ledger finding is a confirmed supply-chain compromise of seed-phrase handling in widely used hardware wallets, and Trezor's decision shows the incident is already reshaping how wallet makers manage third-party distribution.

Ledger says the CryptoBilis implant reads the screen and decodes the recovery phrase when it appears, then sends it out over LTE via an eSIM, without altering the key-storage chip or the operating system, so a tampered wallet looks and works normally. Onchain investigator Specter traced more than $86 million in suspected thefts, a figure Ledger says it has not verified; Trezor has not said how many resellers are affected, and those cut can keep selling existing stock through the holiday season.

Solana consensus upgrade Alpenglow to go live on mainnet on October 21, cutting finality to about 150 milliseconds

Background

Background

TowerBFT has been Solana's consensus mechanism since mainnet launch, working alongside Proof of History (PoH), a cryptographic clock that orders events. Anza and Solana describe Alpenglow as the most significant overhaul of Solana's core protocol to date; the new voting protocol Votor ships in Agave 4.3.

Anza, Solana's core development team, announced that the Alpenglow consensus upgrade is scheduled to activate on Solana mainnet-beta around October 21 at 22:50 (UTC+8, roughly 14:50 UTC), with the migration to be completed within a single slot. Alpenglow replaces TowerBFT, the consensus protocol Solana has used since genesis, and is intended to cut transaction finality from seconds to about 150 milliseconds.

Anza says moving voting off-chain frees block space for user transactions, and that faster finality shortens settlement for institutions from the usual 1–3 business days to roughly 150 milliseconds. It is described as the most significant change to Solana's core consensus since the network launched.

Anza developed Alpenglow over 18 months; it was previously migrated on testnet (September 24) and devnet (September 25) and tested in a 1,500-node simulation, with a bug bounty exceeding 25,000 SOL. Under the new Votor voting protocol, validators exchange votes directly instead of submitting them as on-chain transactions, which the announcement says could cause a significant decline in reported transaction totals that include validator votes.

Impact analysis

The upgrade directly concerns SOL stakers and validators and the applications settling on Solana, since sub-second finality could change how exchanges, custodians and payment processors treat on-chain settlement. Because validator votes move off-chain, widely cited Solana transaction-count metrics may shift, affecting how data providers and market observers read network activity.

XRPN took shareholder vote with no knowledge of XRP inflation bug

Background

Background

XRP is a pre-mined digital asset whose full 100 billion supply was created at the XRP Ledger's launch in 2012, and the protocol has no mining or staking mechanism for issuing new tokens. Evernorth Holdings, an XRP treasury company backed by Ripple and Arrington Capital, completed its business combination with Armada Acquisition Corp. and, per search results, is capitalized with roughly 473 million XRP and $300 million in gross cash proceeds.

A newly disclosed bug in the XRP Ledger's base-layer payment engine could have allowed an attacker to mint trillions of new XRP for almost nothing; researcher Cayden Liao and Veria AI reported it on September 22, and Ripple shipped a closed-source fix in xrpld 3.4.1 on September 25 described only as "integer-arithmetic hardening." Shareholders of the Ripple/Arrington-backed XRP treasury vehicle Evernorth (XRPN) voted on their plan without being told about the flaw, and an official report admitting what the patch actually fixed appeared only on October 9, the day the merger closed.

The flaw meant XRP's fixed supply of 100 billion — the core claim underpinning Evernorth's treasury plan — was technically unenforceable on the network for roughly a decade. The episode also raises governance questions, since XRPN shareholders made redemption and merger decisions inside a window in which Ripple knew about the bug but they did not.

According to the report, the bug likely dated back to 2015 and a single validated transaction using the exploit "could have created spendable XRP far beyond the total supply." The patch shipped without an amendment vote, and no actual exploitation of the vulnerability has been reported.

Anthropic warns AI worms are closing in: 15 cheap TV boxes can already run a 26-billion-parameter model

Background

Background

AI-worm research has been building for some time: IBM described the Morris II proof-of-concept worm that targets generative-AI email assistants, and security vendors classify AI worms as malware that injects self-replicating prompts rather than dropping executable files. In June 2026, University of Toronto researchers reported building and testing a proof-of-concept AI-driven worm that used a locally hosted open-weight large language model to reason its way through a network and replicate itself.

Anthropic published research warning that AI could one day spread like a computer worm — autonomously finding networked devices, deploying models on them and using those devices to propagate further, without necessarily needing data centers. In its tests, 15 Android TV boxes priced at about $55 each ran a split 26-billion-parameter Gemma 4 model at only 0.39 tokens per second, Alibaba's Qwen3.5-9B autonomously stood up inference services on 8 of 9 hardware types, and Zhipu's GLM-5.2 completed a find-transfer-run-propagate loop in an isolated simulated network, covering all 8 target nodes in 4 of 5 runs and 7 of 8 in the fifth.

The research puts a concrete hardware floor under autonomous AI propagation, showing that ordinary consumer devices can serve as a compute source, and Anthropic projects that small models suited to cheap devices may reach GLM-5.2's current simulated-propagation capability by the end of 2026.

The demonstrations are bounded: the experiment deliberately opened an unauthenticated debug interface, which greatly reduced the difficulty of intrusion, and GLM-5.2 itself ran on external servers — what was deployed to target devices was the smaller Llama 3.1 8B, so it did not achieve true self-replication independent of external compute. Based on current experimental data, a 9B model might need one to two days to complete one round of replication.
