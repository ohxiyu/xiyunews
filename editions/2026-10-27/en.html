Working with academic communities to build scientific understanding

Background

Background

The local smoothing conjecture was proposed by Christopher Sogge in 1991 and sits at the top of a chain of harmonic-analysis conjectures: it implies the Bochner–Riesz conjecture, which implies the restriction conjecture, which implies the Kakeya conjecture. The Exponential Time Hypothesis posits that 3-SAT cannot be solved in sub-exponential time and is widely used to derive hardness results, including in parameterized complexity. Yang–Mills existence and mass gap is one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems set by the Clay Mathematics Institute in 2000.

OpenAI announced on October 26, 2026 that its models produced natural-language proofs of three long-standing open results: local smoothing for the Kakeya conjecture, the falsity of the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH), and the existence of quantum chromodynamics and completions of the Standard Model. The Yang–Mills existence proof was generated on September 9, 2026 and the QCD proof on September 18, 2026, with the release held back until Lean formalization of the Yang–Mills result was completed on October 16, 2026.

OpenAI framed the release around how researchers can verify and build on the results, and said the volume and complexity of the work was why publication waited on Lean formalization. Commenters with complexity-theory background treated an ETH refutation as the most consequential item, since ETH underpins many hardness and lower-bound results.

The local smoothing claim is presented as settling Kakeya and Stein's restriction conjecture in every dimension, a problem open since 1991. No independent verification of the proofs has been reported; coverage at the time of the announcement described the results as still unverified.

Background

Under the integrity provisions, NBA players, team and league personnel are barred from trading NBA event contracts, and Kalshi and Polymarket must help the league identify suspicious trading, prevent insider trading and share data in investigations. CFTC Chairman Michael S. Keystone Breach Exposed 25,964 Purchase Records and Compromised Device Verificat

Background

Background

Keystone confirmed the suspected breach of customer shipping information on X on Saturday, roughly 18 hours after a customer reported a social-engineering call in which Keystone order data was used. The company had previously promised that shipping data is permanently deleted 180 days after an order, and the user who first flagged the potential breach said their device was purchased in 2024. The incident adds to a run of hardware-wallet data and supply-chain problems, including phishing emails sent from Foundation Passport's official domain after compromised Cloudflare API keys, and earlier Ledger-related incidents.

Keystone said its investigation found that attackers who breached its website obtained the WebAuth device-authentication key used to verify that a hardware wallet is genuine, potentially allowing counterfeit devices to pass official authenticity checks. The company will destroy 18,835 unsold devices built with the old key, pause unshipped orders and move new production to a replacement key, while the incident affected 25,964 purchase records and 12,873 incomplete checkout records.

The compromised key undermines the authenticity check customers rely on to confirm a device is genuine, even though Keystone said seed phrases, private keys and transaction signing were not compromised. The exposed purchase data — names, contact details, shipping addresses and order details — creates phishing risk, and one customer had already reported a social-engineering call that used Keystone order information.

Keystone said the affected records were limited to purchases made through Shopify on its official site, and that its automated data-deletion tool "did not operate reliably," so some personal information was retained beyond the promised period; it also said another roughly 25,000 records had already been permanently erased under its retention policy.

Background

Under the plan, the two-year one-way migration portal opens after the proposal passes and audits complete, converting AKT 1:1 as SPL tokens with unchanged code, decimals and total supply. The compute marketplace cutover date has not been set and requires a separate governance vote after audits and migration drills; existing workloads would run 90 days after that switch before the old chain halts, with staking rewards ending at cutover and validator roles and revenue ending when the old chain stops.
